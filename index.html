ENGINE

Computational Physiology Engine

Evidence-Based Physiology, Computed in Real Time

Clinical Problem Every clinical tool downstream — simulators, training, decision support — needs a source of truth for how a body behaves over time. Off-the-shelf approaches don't give you a physiology you can compute, audit, and build on.

A physiologically grounded engine that computes a patient's trajectory over time. Vitals, labs, and drug response evolve over time, grounded in published physiology and pharmacology. Every case presentation is distinct, and the underlying state is fully auditable.

Architecture & capabilities +
  • Multi-agent physiology engine grounded in published literature
  • GPU-accelerated batch computation
  • Evidence-based pharmacokinetic library
  • Clinical scoring: SOFA, qSOFA, NEWS2, GCS
  • Core ML surrogate export for on-device inference
Python PyTorch CUDA asyncio Core ML
SIMULATOR

Body Simulator

Clinical Simulator Front-End

Clinical Problem Bedside-quality patient simulators are locked into expensive vendor mannequins tied to a single training room. Every discipline that trains on patients needs a software-only simulator driven by real physiology, not scripted playback.

A clinical front-end wired live to the physiology engine over a WebSocket bridge. Vitals, four-channel waveforms, and drug response render from computed engine state in real time, with a graceful fallback when the bridge is offline.

Architecture & capabilities +
  • Live WebSocket bridge to the engine
  • 4-channel waveforms: ECG, arterial, pleth, capno
  • Real-time vitals and pharmacological response
  • Code-split bundle for fast load
  • Graceful fallback when bridge unavailable
React TypeScript WebSocket Canvas

Simulated physiology for training and research. It does not display or interpret data from a real patient.

TRAINING SIM

PatientSim

AI-Driven Clinical Training Simulator

Clinical Problem Sim-based education is expensive, schedule-dependent, and doesn't scale. Clinicians across every discipline need on-demand, realistic case exposure, especially for low-frequency, high-acuity events.

Standalone simulation with AI-driven evolving clinical narratives, real-time vitals, and a set of interactive procedures. Built for ED education days with Sentry error tracking and keyboard shortcuts for rapid clinical input.

Architecture & capabilities +
  • Dynamic vitals with physiological drift engine
  • SVG ECG driven by a rhythm library: AFib, VT/VF, torsades, AV block and paced rhythms each render their own morphology, not a rate-shifted sinus trace
  • AI-driven case evolution based on clinical decisions
  • Interactive procedures with SBAR generation
  • Sound alerts and keyboard shortcuts for rapid input
React SVG Gemini AI

Training only. Every patient is simulated. No real patient data enters it.

CONSUMER iOS

Meridian

Kymora, Consumer iOS Application

Consumer Problem Patients on complex medication regimens have no consumer-grade tool that pairs personal med tracking with a physiologically grounded model of how those drugs interact with their own biometrics.

Native iOS application pairing on-device medication tracking with a Core ML surrogate of the physiological simulation engine, so drug-context awareness runs locally, without sending biometric data to a server.

Architecture & capabilities +
  • Native SwiftUI app targeting iOS 17+
  • HealthKit integration for biometric context
  • Core ML surrogate exported from the physiological engine
  • Local-first: on-device computation, privacy-preserving
  • Medication list organization and reminders, entered by label or barcode, no visual pill identification, which is ruled out by design
Swift SwiftUI HealthKit Core ML
Scaffolded · development deferred

Organization and context only. No dosing, no recommendations, no interpretation of biometrics.

Clinical Story Mode

Clinical Teaching Articles, Rebuilt as Living Cases

Education Problem An article teaches what to know. It can't show what it feels like to catch it at the bedside — the vitals moving, the waveform changing, the decisions landing in the right order under time pressure.

Each clinical teaching article (grounded in peer-reviewed literature) re-staged as a real-time patient case. A live monitor tracks the patient as you scroll: heart rate, blood pressure, and an ECG that morphs with the physiology, while the clinical reasoning surfaces exactly when the case reaches it. The same evidence base as the article library, delivered as something you move through instead of read.

CLINICAL PROTOTYPE · LIVE DEMO · OPEN SOURCE

Trauma Tracker

A Real-Time ED Trauma Registrar

Clinical Problem A trauma resuscitation generates an enormous amount of documentable fact in a very short window, and everyone in the room is busy. The record gets reconstructed afterward, from memory, by someone who was also running the resuscitation — which is where timestamps drift and quality measures get missed.

Built at the July 2026 Abridge, Anthropic and Lightspeed hackathon, with a physician teammate auditing clinical accuracy. Documentation runs during the resuscitation: the nursing narrative and the accreditation clocks come from one set of timestamped events, and any question nobody answered stays in the report as an open item.

LIVE · RUNS IN THE BROWSER
Architecture & capabilities +
  • Values and interventions extracted and stamped as they are spoken
  • Shock Index, RTS and GCS computed from published scoring tables
  • Activation determination carries the evidence it was made on
  • Unresolved prompts appear in the output as documented gaps
  • Four capture sources · synthetic data only
React Multi-Agent MIT

Four more, not linked

Each came out of something watched breaking at the bedside. None is finished enough to hand you.

Why there is nothing to click They touch clinical decision-making and none is past prototype. You cannot verify any of it from here; weight it accordingly.
Architecture & capabilities +
  • UnitScheduler Pro, reads historical schedules and surfaces the coverage pattern a static grid hides. Advisory only: a coverage suggestion is a draft for the charge nurse and never an assignment. Whether it changes an outcome is unmeasured.
  • Emergent Media Manager: an outside EKG on paper, a CD of imaging, a scanned discharge summary: all carry clinical meaning, none of it queryable. Turns those into structured records, local-first, with an egress guard that blocks identifier patterns from reaching a cloud model. Prototype, synthetic data only. The guard detects and blocks; it does not de-identify, and that is not a compliance claim.

  • Alerter, concept stage, a design for ordering alerts by acuity context rather than firing them all at one weight. Nothing is built. The constraint that it may never withhold an alert from the clinician is the part worth stating.
