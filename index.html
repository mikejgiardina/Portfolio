ENGINE

Computational Physiology Engine

Evidence-Based Physiology, Computed in Real Time

Clinical Problem Simulators, training cases and decision-support tools all need a model of how a patient changes over time.

A model of a patient that runs forward in time, grounded in published physiology and pharmacology. Vitals, labs and drug response change with what happens to the patient and with what the learner does, and each model component cites the published work it is based on.

Architecture & capabilities +
  • Each model component cites the published work it is based on
  • GPU-accelerated batch computation
  • Drug behavior modeled from published pharmacology
  • Clinical scores computed from the model: SOFA, qSOFA, NEWS2, GCS
  • A simplified version can be exported to run on a phone
Python engine
SIMULATOR

Body Simulator

Clinical Simulator Front-End

Clinical Problem Mannequin simulation needs a dedicated room and a booked time. A screen-based simulator running on a physiology model is an alternative to a dedicated mannequin room.

The screen a learner works from, connected live to the physiology engine. The monitor's vitals and four waveforms are calculated from the engine's current state, and a drug given in the simulator changes what the engine calculates next. If the connection drops, the screen switches to a simplified fallback.

Architecture & capabilities +
  • Live connection to the physiology engine
  • Four waveforms: ECG, arterial line, pleth, capnography
  • Vitals and drug response update in real time
  • Switches to a simplified fallback if the engine connection drops
Web

Simulated physiology for training and research. It does not display or interpret data from a real patient.

TRAINING SIM

PatientSim

AI-Driven Clinical Training Simulator

Clinical Problem Simulation days are expensive and hard to schedule, and the rare, high-acuity cases are the ones staff get the least practice on.

A standalone training simulator with its own simpler physiology model. Vitals change over time as the case runs, the case changes with what the learner does, and an AI model plays the patient. Built for ED education days.

Architecture & capabilities +
  • Vitals change over time as the case runs
  • ECG rhythm library: AFib, VT/VF, torsades, AV blocks and paced rhythms each draw their own shape, not a sped-up sinus trace
  • The case changes based on the learner's decisions
  • Procedures, with an SBAR handoff generated at the end
  • Alarm sounds and keyboard shortcuts for fast input
Web

Training only. Every patient is simulated. No real patient data enters it.

CONSUMER iOS

Meridian

Kymora, Consumer iOS Application

Consumer Problem People on several medications can keep a list in an app, but nothing connects that list to how those drugs behave in the body.

An iPhone app designed to keep the medication list on the phone and pair it with a simplified version of the physiology model running on the phone, so health data would not need to go to a server.

Architecture & capabilities +
  • Planned as an iPhone app for iOS 17 and later
  • Designed to read Apple Health data for context
  • Designed to run a simplified version of the physiology model on the phone
  • Designed to keep health data on the device
  • Designed to take medication entries by label or barcode. Identifying pills from a photo is ruled out by design.
iOS / Swift
Early stage · on hold

Organization and context only. No dosing, no recommendations, no interpretation of biometrics.

EDUCATION · INTERACTIVE · LIVE

Clinical Story Mode

Clinical Teaching Articles, Rebuilt as Living Cases

Education Problem An article teaches what to know. It can't show what it feels like to catch it at the bedside: the vitals moving, the waveform changing, the decisions coming in the right order under time pressure.

Each clinical teaching article (grounded in peer-reviewed literature) re-staged as a real-time patient case. A live monitor tracks the patient as you scroll: heart rate, blood pressure, and an ECG that morphs with the physiology, while the clinical reasoning surfaces exactly when the case reaches it. The sources are the same ones the article cites.

  • ECG and waveforms that change with the pathology
  • Embedded decision points and interactive teaching tools
  • Same evidence base as the article library
  • Runs in a web browser, including on a phone

CLINICAL PROTOTYPE · LIVE DEMO · OPEN SOURCE

Trauma Tracker

A Real-Time ED Trauma Registrar

Clinical Problem A trauma resuscitation generates an enormous amount of documentable fact in a very short window, and everyone in the room is busy. The record gets reconstructed afterward, from memory, by someone who was also running the resuscitation — which is where timestamps drift and quality measures get missed.

Built at the July 2026 Abridge, Anthropic and Lightspeed hackathon, with a physician teammate auditing clinical accuracy. Documentation runs during the resuscitation: the nursing narrative and the accreditation clocks come from one set of timestamped events, and any question nobody answered stays in the report as an open item.

LIVE · RUNS IN THE BROWSER
Architecture & capabilities +
  • Values and interventions extracted and stamped as they are spoken
  • Shock Index, RTS and GCS computed from published scoring tables
  • The activation level is shown with the findings it was based on
  • Unanswered questions stay in the report as open items
  • Four input sources · synthetic data only
Web Open source (MIT)

PROTOTYPE · RECORDED WALKTHROUGH

Nurse Orchestrator

Contextual Clinical Decision Support

Clinical Problem General AI assistants aren't set up for bedside use. They don't know unit policy or where a nurse's scope of practice ends in a given state.

A clinical assistant that works out what kind of question is being asked and applies guardrails based on published scope-of-practice frameworks for the asker's state, with the policy source shown. The guardrails work in the demo; whether they hold against someone trying to get around them is untested.

Architecture & capabilities +
  • Answers adjusted to the clinician's discipline
  • Sorts each question by what is being asked and routes it accordingly
  • Scope-of-practice limits by state
  • Looks up the relevant policy and shows the source, so a clinician can check the basis

ALSO BUILT · PROTOTYPES, NOT LINKED

Four more, not linked

Each came out of something watched breaking at the bedside. None is finished enough to hand you.

Why there is nothing to click They touch clinical decision-making and none is past prototype. You cannot verify any of it from here; weight it accordingly.
Architecture & capabilities +
  • UnitScheduler Pro, reads historical schedules and surfaces the coverage pattern a static grid hides. Advisory only: a coverage suggestion is a draft for the charge nurse and never an assignment. Whether it changes an outcome is unmeasured.
  • Emergent Media Manager: an outside EKG on paper, a CD of imaging, a scanned discharge summary: all carry clinical meaning, none of it queryable. Turns those into structured records, processed locally first, with a filter that blocks recognizable identifier patterns from being sent to a cloud AI model. Prototype, synthetic data only. The filter blocks; it does not de-identify, and that is not a compliance claim.
  • Clinical Information Workflow Engine, drafts CE-style articles and simulation scenarios in the structure nurse educators work in. Everything it produces is a draft for a human reviewer. Not an accreditation claim and not peer review.
  • Alerter, concept stage, a design for ordering alerts by acuity context rather than firing them all at one weight. Nothing is built. The constraint that it may never withhold an alert from the clinician is the part worth stating.
